standard error above. Read the sign AND the interval, not the sign alone.
the curve reverses direction 2 times across 7 half-lives; a real recency
effect would fall monotonically as more of the past is let in.


VERDICT

1. AGREEMENT. Seasons agree with each other at 0.264 within the old era and
   0.293 within the recent one; across the split they agree at 0.280.
   The across number is not below both - no era structure in how plans rank.
   Agreement against the gap between seasons: -0.0112 +/- 0.0056 a year (2.01 sigma).
   It decays with distance - the past MAY be less relevant.

2. TRANSFER. Fitting on the recent seasons instead of the old ones is worth
   +29.7 +/- 31.1 points a season on held-out seasons.
   That is inside the noise: no measurable penalty for learning from the old seasons.

3. COST OF HEDGING. Flat weighting is worth 13.0 effective seasons; the best
   half-life on held-out seasons (8) is worth 11.8, and beats flat by +60.7 +/- 33.8.
   That winner reverses the curve 2 times out of 6, so it is the best of seven noisy draws, not a recency effect.

RECOMMENDATION: BORDERLINE. Pool all 13 seasons, or hedge gently. Do NOT cut.
The strongest era signal is 2.01 standard errors, which is exactly the region
where a rule should not pretend to decide. Both estimates lean the same way -
the past is slightly less relevant - and neither survives a hard look: the
drift interval is optimistic (its pairs are not independent), and the weighting
grid's winner reverses the curve 2 times.

The cheap hedge is the LONGEST half-life, 21 seasons: it costs 0.2 effective
seasons out of 13.0. A cutoff year is NOT the same thing and is not supported -
there is no year in this data where anything changes.

What this does NOT say: that the game has not changed. It says a plan fitted on
2013-2018 does not measurably lose on 2021-2025 IN THIS GAME - eleven rounds with
a quarterback and a back already in hand. Run -PnoKeepers and the answer changes,
which is the point: the keeper structure removes the quarterback timing decision,
and that decision is where the eras differ most.
