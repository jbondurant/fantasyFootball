A weighting only earns its complexity if it beats flat by more than the
standard error above. Read the sign AND the interval, not the sign alone.
the curve reverses direction 3 times across 7 half-lives; a real recency
effect would fall monotonically as more of the past is let in.


VERDICT

1. AGREEMENT. Seasons agree with each other at 0.210 within the old era and
   0.213 within the recent one; across the split they agree at 0.159.
   The across number is BELOW both - the eras do rank plans differently.
   Agreement against the gap between seasons: -0.0143 +/- 0.0051 a year (2.80 sigma).
   It decays with distance - the past MAY be less relevant.

2. TRANSFER. Fitting on the recent seasons instead of the old ones is worth
   +89.5 +/- 31.7 points a season on held-out seasons.
   That is outside the noise: the old seasons teach a measurably worse lesson.

3. COST OF HEDGING. Flat weighting is worth 13.0 effective seasons; the best
   half-life on held-out seasons (8) is worth 11.8, and beats flat by +0.0 +/- 0.0.
   That winner reverses the curve 3 times out of 6, so it is the best of seven noisy draws, not a recency effect.

RECOMMENDATION: WEIGHT, do not pool. Use a half-life of 8 seasons.
The old seasons are still evidence - a cutoff throws them away entirely, which
costs more resolution than their bias is worth - but they should not count fully.

What this does NOT say: that the game has not changed. It says a plan fitted on
2013-2018 does not measurably lose on 2021-2025 IN THIS GAME - eleven rounds with
a quarterback and a back already in hand. Run -PnoKeepers and the answer changes,
which is the point: the keeper structure removes the quarterback timing decision,
and that decision is where the eras differ most.
