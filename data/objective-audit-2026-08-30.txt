Read the vs-base column against its own +-se, not against a remembered rule of
thumb. The se is PAIRED - the standard error of the season-by-season difference
from the baseline, which is the right test because every variant is scored on
the same five seasons and most of the season-to-season spread cancels. It is
still an n of five: nothing here clears two of its own standard errors, so no
variant in this table has been shown to be better. A changed PLAN is the finding
that survives, because the plan is what gets drafted on Tuesday.

Mean points the trust term adds or removes, by rank band. A plus-or-minus-six
window is nearly flat away from the top of a position, so the whole effect of
the trust coefficient lands on the first few men at each position - and on
defences, where thirty-two players make every window a large share of the
position. Everywhere else the coefficient could be anything at all.

The seam column is what membership of the CSV is worth. It runs one way: the
drafted man is in the file and keeps ~92% of his projection, the replacement
man is not and keeps ~82%, so every skill marginal is inflated against its own
replacement. Defences are in neither table and keep 100%, which is literally
true - a defence does not tear an achilles - and is still the largest single
cross-position asymmetry in the objective.

On the functional form: games played and points per game correlate +0.35 at
running back and +0.67 at quarterback (OutcomeDistributions), so a man who
misses games also scores less in the ones he plays, and a linear (17-g)/17
understates him. That is NOT a licence to bend the curve. The correlation is
measured on realised seasons, where playing badly CAUSES the benching that
cuts games played - the arrow runs backwards from what a projected absence
would need. Leave it linear.
