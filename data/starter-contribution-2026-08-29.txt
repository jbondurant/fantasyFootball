YOUR TWO WORLDS, AND WHERE THEY LAND

The idealised corner - nobody hurt, nobody busting - is the top left cell,
and it reads -3.2. That is a tie. So the intuition is right exactly where it
was stated: strip out failure and the tight end is as good a use of the pick
as anything else, because a bench player really does contribute nothing.

But it never gets better than a tie, and every step away from that corner
makes it worse - monotonically, in both directions. Injuries and busts push
the same way, which is why the second dial did not rescue it: a bust starter
is a slot a bench man takes without anyone getting hurt, so it is the same
mechanism arriving by a different road. At the measured world, 1.0x and 1.0x,
the tight end is 43.6 points behind.

The reason it cannot do better than a tie even at the corner is the FLEX.
The intuition assumes the extra receiver is a bench player. He is not: two
flex slots mean a fourth receiver or third back starts every week regardless.
He is a starter bought at pick 79. And the slot the tight end fills is not
empty either - the waiver wire fills it nearly as well for free, which is the
streaming result arriving from the other side.

So the honest summary is not that failure decides this. Failure widens a gap
that is already there at zero. What decides it is the lineup shape: two flex
slots and a startable tight end on the wire.
