Sampling changes the magnitudes and steadies the tail. The scalar model swung
from -66.8 to +16.3 and back across three adjacent picks, which was one
realized season showing through; drawing from a tier's distribution smooths
that into a monotone curve. Both marginals rise, because dispersion creates
starts - and the alternative rises more, because a receiver has a flex slot
waiting and a tight end does not.

Read this as WHICH position for one pick, not WHEN to take the tight end.
The roster here is fixed at the six early picks plus one candidate, so it
never asks what happens after you have taken four receivers and the flex is
full. TightEndTiming's swap is the sequential question and answers 'when';
this answers 'what is this one pick worth'. They agree that 79 is too early
and disagree about how much better 90 is - under sampling, 90 is barely an
improvement on 79, and the gap only really closes past 127.

YOUR TWO WORLDS, AND WHERE THEY LAND

The idealised corner - nobody hurt, nobody busting - is the top left cell,
and it reads -36.3. So even with failure switched off entirely the tight
end loses, and the intuition does not survive contact with this lineup.

At the measured world - 1.0x injuries, 1.0x busts - the gap is -55.3.
Both dials push the same way, which is why the second did not rescue it: a
bust starter is a slot a bench man takes without anyone getting hurt, so it
is the same mechanism arriving by a different road.

What stops the tight end even at the corner is the FLEX. The intuition assumes
the extra receiver is a bench player. He is not: two flex slots mean a fourth
receiver or third back starts every week regardless, so he is a starter bought
at pick 79, and his marginal never falls to the wire the way a tight end's
does.

The pick sweep above is the part that moves: the gap closes steadily as the
pick moves back, because a tight end barely gets worse with the rounds while
a receiver falls off a cliff. That is the plateau, and it is real - it just
does not reach zero before the ADP data thins out.
