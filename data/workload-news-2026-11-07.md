## Ashton Jeanty (RB, Las Vegas Raiders)

The reporting does show a reduced workload, and it came in two stages. First, his carries fell or held steady every week, from 23 in Week 1 to 10 in Weeks 5 and 6, while he played through an ankle sprain from late August and a later foot problem. Then in Week 8 at the Jets he got 2 carries and a season-low 40% of snaps, while rookie Mike Washington Jr. took 25 carries and 60%. His snap share did not fall in a straight line: about 76% over Weeks 1-4, 53% in Week 5, 58% in Week 6 and 72% in Week 7. Week 7 was a 35-21 loss in which he still ran only 8 times, so the clear cut in snaps is one game old (Week 8, played Nov 1). Washington now gets the early downs and the carries inside the 10. Jeanty keeps third downs, two-minute work and passing downs, and he leads NFL running backs in catches and receiving yards. Coach Klint Kubiak said the Week 8 split was his call, that it was set before kickoff and was not about Jeanty's pregame comments, and that the split will be decided week to week. He also said he 'absolutely' believes Jeanty can still be a bell-cow back, and on Nov 6 he would only say that all three backs will play at San Francisco. ESPN's Dan Graziano reported on Nov 4 that the team no longer blames the ankle, since Jeanty has practiced fully every week. Per Graziano, the team wants Jeanty to play better, sees Washington as more explosive, and will lean the carries toward Washington if Jeanty does not respond. No source gives injury, the offensive line or a play-caller change as the reason for Week 8. Analysts point to his 3.1 yards per carry, one analyst's EPA ranking that has him as the least efficient RB, and turnover at right guard; they expect him to keep the passing-down role and call him a low-end RB2. Nothing reported commits to giving his early-down work back, and the Week 9 game at the 49ers (Nov 8) has not been played.

## Rhamondre Stevenson (New England Patriots, RB)

The reporting does not show Stevenson's workload shrinking. Through Week 8 he has 96 carries and 27 targets (ESPN game log), and his snap share has risen in each of the last three games: 60% in Week 6, about 68-69% in Week 7, and 76-77% in Week 8. Week 8 also brought a season-high 19 carries, with TreVeyon Henderson out ill. His carry count swings a lot from week to week (6 to 19). The low games had clear causes: in Week 2 an opening-drive fumble shifted the work to Henderson, Week 3 was a 35-6 blowout loss, and in Week 7 New England trailed and used him mostly as a receiver (6 carries, but 5 catches for 93 yards). Henderson has missed or been limited for much of the year: a camp ankle injury cost him Week 1, a high-ankle sprain hit him in Week 5, and he was then hospitalized with an illness involving unexplained neurological symptoms. He missed Week 8, is officially out for Week 9 against Green Bay, and no return timetable has been reported. Corey Kiner is the other active back. Mike Vrabel rode him as "the hot hand" late in Week 6, and he had 7 carries in Week 8. On the team side, patriots.com wrote before Week 8 that it expected Stevenson's heavy workload to continue. Vrabel praised his pass protection, toughness and ball security after the Miami win, and Globe beat writer Nicole Yang reported that Stevenson keeps the edge on third downs and pass protection. Goal-line volume is thin for the whole team: RotoWire says New England has only 3 rush attempts inside the 5 all season, second-fewest in the league. Analyst opinion is split. Draft Sharks called him a sell candidate after Week 7. Fantasy Life rates him a borderline RB2 with Henderson playing and a mid-range RB2 without him. Hayden Winks' numbers show his usage and output much better when Henderson is out. Two open risks: the November 10 trade deadline, where reports link the Patriots to Jonathan Taylor, and Henderson's eventual return. Note that the reduced-workload worry in the question is about Jeanty; this research covered only Stevenson.
